The meaning of sakura

Cherry blossoms are among Japan's most recognizable symbols. Their delicate flowers appear in great numbers, then disappear almost as quickly as they arrived.

That brief life has made sakura a lasting image of beauty, renewal, and impermanence. Every spring, people pause beneath the trees to appreciate a moment that cannot be held forever.

Stories from blossom season

Tradition

Hanami beneath the trees

Families, friends, and coworkers gather for picnics beneath flowering branches, sharing food while the petals drift overhead.

Evening

The glow of yozakura

At night, lanterns illuminate the blossoms. These evening celebrations are known as yozakura, or night sakura.

Travel

Following the sakura front

Because Japan stretches across several climates, the bloom moves northward over time, creating a seasonal “cherry blossom front.”

A simple hanami guide

Arrive early, bring a picnic mat, respect posted park rules, and take all rubbish with you. Popular locations can become busy, so quieter neighborhood parks may offer an equally memorable view.

Most importantly, look up. The finest part of hanami is often the simplest: sitting still while spring changes above you.
